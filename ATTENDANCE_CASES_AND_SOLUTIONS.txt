              HOW OUR SOLUTION HANDLES CASES & ERRORS IN PLAIN ENGLISH

1. THE STANDARD HAPPY PATH (NORMAL DAY-TO-DAY CASE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Here is what happens on a completely normal, smooth working day:

Step 1: Arrival & Check-In
  - Freelancer reaches the wedding/event hall.
  - Exactly 30 minutes before the event start time, the app opens.
  - Because they granted Chrome location access, the app detects they entered the 
    venue (e.g., within 150 meters).
  - Check-in happens AUTOMATICALLY in the background. A green badge pops up: 
    "Welcome to [Event Name]! Auto Check-In Successful."
  - If they prefer, they can also simply tap the "Check In" button.

Step 2: Working the Shift
  - The freelancer works their standard shift (e.g., 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM).
  - The app displays active shift status and earnings in progress.

Step 3: On-Time Checkout
  - At 1:00 PM, the event finishes on schedule.
  - The freelancer taps "Stop Shift / Check Out".
  - Because there was no overtime, checkout happens instantly.
  - No photos required, no complex steps. Shift completed, payment calculated.


================================================================================
2. HOW OUR SOLUTION SOLVES REAL-WORLD ERRORS & EDGE CASES
================================================================================

CASE 1: "Freelancer tries to check in yesterday, tomorrow, or hours too early"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Problem:
  Freelancers accidentally or dishonestly tap check-in from their house the night 
  before or hours before the event starts.
* Our Solution:
  - Both the backend server and frontend lock the check-in button.
  - Check-in is strictly blocked until 30 minutes before the scheduled Start Time 
    on the exact day of the event.
  - If someone tries early, the server rejects it with error: 
    "Check-in locked. Opens 30 minutes before event start."
  - UI shows a countdown: "🔒 Opens at 8:30 AM (30 mins prior to shift)".


CASE 2: "Freelancer denies Chrome GPS permission or phone GPS is weak"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Problem:
  Chrome doesn't have background location permission, or user is in a basement hall 
  where auto-detection takes too long.
* Our Solution:
  - The system NEVER relies only on auto-detection.
  - A bright manual "Check In" button is always present on the screen as fallback.
  - When the user taps the manual button, it takes a fresh one-shot GPS reading.
  - If GPS is completely unavailable indoors, they can tap "Report Exception" 
    so the admin can manually approve their attendance.


CASE 3: "Freelancer checks in from home or 5 kilometers away from venue"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Problem:
  Worker claims they are at the event, but they are actually stuck in traffic or at home.
* Our Solution:
  - The server calculates the exact distance (in meters) between the worker's GPS 
    and the venue coordinates using the Haversine formula.
  - If distance is greater than the venue radius (e.g. 150m):
    The check-in is NOT accepted as verified. Instead, it gets marked as 
    "MANUAL_REVIEW" with an alert to the Admin: 
    "Worker checked in 3.4 km away from venue".
  - Admin decides whether to approve or reject the hours.


CASE 4: "Shift ends, worker forgets to check out and goes home"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Problem:
  At 1:00 PM, the shift ends. The worker packs their cameras, leaves the venue, 
  and forgets to tap "Check Out" until midnight. Should they get paid 11 hours overtime?
* Our Solution:
  - At the exact scheduled End Time, a 30-Minute Grace Countdown pops up on screen.
  - If the worker walks away and takes NO ACTION for 30 minutes:
    The system AUTOMATICALLY STOPS and checks out the shift.
  - The checkout time is recorded strictly as: Scheduled End Time + 30 minutes.
  - An audit log is saved: "Shift automatically stopped after 30-minute grace period".
  - The studio owner does NOT get overcharged for forgotten checkouts.


CASE 5: "Event runs late and worker actually needs to work overtime"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Problem:
  The wedding reception gets delayed and runs 2 hours extra. The worker needs to 
  stay and work, not get auto-stopped.
* Our Solution:
  - When the End Time arrives and the 30-minute grace modal appears, the worker 
    simply taps "EXTEND SHIFT".
  - The 30-minute auto-stop timer is immediately CANCELLED.
  - The shift transitions into "⚡ Live Overtime Mode".
  - A live counter ticks on screen showing current overtime (+45m, +1h 15m, etc.).
  - WE DO NOT ASK FOR PHOTOS YET, because the overtime work has just started!


CASE 6: "Worker finishes overtime and clicks Checkout (Proof of Work)"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Problem:
  After working 2 hours extra, the worker wants their overtime pay. The studio owner 
  needs proof that the worker was actually still there working, not drinking tea outside.
* Our Solution:
  - When the worker taps "Check Out" at the conclusion of their overtime:
  - The system detects: "Overtime Detected (+2 hours)".
  - A modal pops up: "Shift Finished - Please snap a photo of the completed venue/stage".
  - The camera opens directly. The worker snaps a photo of the stage/hall.
  - The photo is uploaded to Cloudinary.
  - The shift completes, and the overtime claim is submitted with the attached proof.


CASE 7: "Worker tries to upload an old photo from yesterday or a saved screenshot"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Problem:
  Worker wants overtime money, so they upload a photo they took yesterday or a downloaded picture.
* Our Solution (EXIF Shutter Timestamp):
  - Every photo taken by a phone camera embeds raw EXIF metadata inside the file.
  - Our system automatically extracts the `DateTimeOriginal` tag (the exact second 
    the camera sensor snapped the image).
  - If someone picks yesterday's photo, the EXIF timestamp immediately exposes that 
    it was taken yesterday, not tonight!
  - Furthermore, our server records the exact UTC timestamp the upload hit our servers, 
    so changing the phone clock does not fool the system.


CASE 8: "Android phone strips the GPS from the photo for privacy"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Problem:
  Modern Android OS (Android 11 to 14) sometimes removes GPS location tags from photos 
  for privacy protection.
* Our Solution (Dual-Layer Fallback):
  - Layer 1: We attempt to read the photo's embedded EXIF GPS tags.
  - Layer 2: At the exact second the photo is snapped, our app automatically takes a 
    high-accuracy GPS reading from the Chrome browser.
  - The photo is bundled with this live verified location.
  - Result: Even if Android deletes the photo's internal GPS, our system STILL captures 
    the exact venue coordinates where the worker was standing.


CASE 9: "Phone battery dies or network connection drops"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Problem:
  Worker's phone shuts down or there is zero internet in a remote resort.
* Our Solution:
  - The "Report Attendance Exception" feature allows workers to submit missed 
    punches as soon as they reconnect or get home.
  - The request includes the reason (e.g., "Phone died", "No network in basement").
  - The Admin reviews the incident against event logs and can approve the hours with 1 click.
